## The Royal Ploughing Ceremony

In the set: painting 7 of 12
Ceremony of: Sixth month, paired with Visakha Bucha
Scene: The ploughing ground
Located on: the wall of the main vihara, Wat Rajpradit

![The Royal Ploughing Ceremony](mural.jpg)

The seventh ceremony in the set is Visakha Bucha, marking the Buddha's birth,
enlightenment and passing. Instituted under Rama II and held in the sixth month,
it saw nobles and officials set out blue-and-white porcelain tables; Rama IV
later replaced these with rank lanterns hung inside Wat Phra Kaew, the royal
lantern hanging on the ordination hall doors.

This mural paints the other rite of the same month, the Royal Ploughing
Ceremony, held as the rainy season opens to bless the farmers. Unlike the
others it had no fixed date.

The Lord of the Harvest grips the plough beneath a tiered umbrella, ringed by
attendants with regalia and musicians, behind a pair of sacred oxen. He ploughs
three oval rounds, then three broad rounds; four consorts carry silver and gold
baskets for him to scatter the rice, then three more rounds cover it before the
oxen are unyoked to choose from the offerings of augury.
