## The Triyampawai Tripawai Rite

In the set: painting 3 of 12
Ceremony of: Second month
Scene: The Giant Swing, Wat Suthat
Located on: the wall of the main vihara, Wat Rajpradit

![The Triyampawai Tripawai Rite](mural.jpg)

A Brahmin-Hindu rite, held on the belief that two gods - Shiva and Vishnu -
descend to visit the world once a year.

The mural shows the Giant Swing in front of the Phra Sri Sakyamuni hall at Wat
Suthat, thick with onlookers. Four raised pavilions built by Rama IV stand in
view, put there so the king and the nobility could watch the swinging. In front,
a palanquin carries the deity images, surrounded by Brahmins making offerings
and five firework stands lit for the procession into the ceremonial ground.

## The sensor at this painting

Device: dev-C
Placement: mounted beside the painting (edit this file to match the real spot)

It measures temperature, humidity and vibration at the painted wall.
Swinging humidity and sustained vibration are the two things that crack and
lift the paint layer of a mural.
