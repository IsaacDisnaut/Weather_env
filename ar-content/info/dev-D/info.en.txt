## The Chinese New Year Offering

In the set: painting 4 of 12
Ceremony of: Chinese New Year
Scene: The Chao Phraya river
Located on: the wall of the main vihara, Wat Rajpradit

![The Chinese New Year Offering](mural.jpg)

Begun under Rama III, when Chinese residents brought so many offerings that the
king made merit in return - three days of monastic meals, with the wives of
senior officials taking turns to bring boats of khanom chin. Rama IV later
judged khanom chin not to be Chinese food and changed the dish to kao lao.

He also had a Chinese-roofed shrine built, brought the deity images down from
the Ho Kaeo to receive offerings across the three days, had the royal scribe
recite the Lilit Thewaphali, and added Chinese offerings before the Buddha -
watermelon, khanom khaeng, pickled garlic, sugar and oranges.

The mural shows the Chao Phraya with Siamese and Chinese boatmen rowing north,
monks seated at their meal facing the river, and the officials' kao lao boats
drawn up at the landing to take their turn.

## The sensor at this painting

Device: dev-D
Placement: mounted beside the painting (edit this file to match the real spot)

It measures temperature, humidity and vibration at the painted wall.
Swinging humidity and sustained vibration are the two things that crack and
lift the paint layer of a mural.
