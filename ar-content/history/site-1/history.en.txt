## Wat Rajpradit Sathit Maha Simaram

Full name: Wat Rajpradit Sathit Maha Simaram Rajaworawihan
Former name: Wat Rajpradit Sathit Dhammayuttikaram
Rank: First-class royal monastery, Rajaworawihan type
Location: Saranrom Road, Phra Borom Maha Ratchawang, Phra Nakhon, Bangkok
Founder: King Mongkut, Rama IV

King Mongkut bought this plot, an abandoned coffee plantation, with his own
private funds. Ancient royal tradition held that the capital should have three
principal monasteries - Wat Mahathat, Wat Rachaburana and Wat Rajpradit - and
the king also intended this one to serve the Dhammayuttika order he had founded.

![The marble main vihara, built under Rama IV](vihara.jpg)

## Restoration

Restoration work was completed during the reign of King Chulalongkorn, Rama V,
who also commissioned the murals inside the main vihara. Rama VI later had two
decayed timber structures taken down and replaced with two Khmer-style prangs.

![Murals of the twelve-month royal ceremonies, painted under Rama V](murals.jpg)

## Architecture and artwork

Main vihara: marble cladding with an orange tiled roof
Chedi: marble, matching the vihara
Principal Buddha: Phra Buddha Sihangkhapatimakon, after the Phra Buddha Sihing
Murals: the twelve-month royal ceremonies
Pearl inlay: Japanese work of the Nagasaki school, over 156 years old
Prangs: two, housing the image of Rama IV and the Buddhist scriptures

![Inside the main vihara, with the principal Buddha and the murals](interior.jpg)

The pearl-inlay door and window panels were authenticated through Thai-Japanese
cultural research, and count among the Nagasaki-school work surviving in Thailand.

![Pearl-inlay door panels, Japanese work of the Nagasaki school](pearl.jpg)
